# Collector Module Context

Last reviewed: 2026-08-13
Status: Canonical module runtime; backend aliases are retired and browser URLs remain unchanged.

## Purpose and current scope

- Assign collectors to service areas and report assignment/collection state.
- Provide collector-specific customer/payment options and next-due resolution.
- Capture field payments and support receipt reprinting.
- Persist Android collector reschedules, retain their schedule history, and provide branch-scoped Admin create/edit/delete management that syncs to the assigned collector device.
- Persist branch-scoped priority-client collection assignments created by Admin, cache the shared queue offline on every collector's Android dashboard, and retain cancellation/payment/expiration audit history plus the actual collector identity on resulting payment or reschedule records.
- Manage approval queues and approve/reject collected entries.
- Automatically group synced collector payments into daily remittances and provide branch-scoped Admin confirmation/rejection with payment-level approval and audit details.
- Present collection history and reports.

## Backend and APIs

- `backend/index.js` is the lazy module descriptor loaded by `server.js` through `core/runtime/module-loader`.
- `backend/collectors.js`: assignment CRUD/reporting under `/api/collectors`. Monthly collection reporting attributes each positive, approved `payment` credit to the collector identity that recorded it, includes prepaid and postpaid customers, and excludes pending/rejected entries, adjustments, debits, and remittance state. Current area assignments affect area presentation only and never reassign historical collector totals.
- `backend/collector-payments.js`: options, reprints, payment submission, approvals, and remittances under `/api/collector/payments`. Collector submissions are written as `pending_approval` and are excluded by Billing's canonical effective-entry filter until an Admin approves them. Exact retries are idempotent by stable client payment ID when provided and by matching account/reference/amount/date/method/collector details for older Android releases; conflicting reuse returns `409`. MySQL approval locks and changes the pending row plus its reviewer audit in one transaction. Rejection requires a reason, never affects the canonical balance, and retains Admin/time/reason audit metadata. Successful uploads are idempotently grouped by collector and collection day into pending remittances. Customer-payment decisions and cash remittance are separate controls: remittance confirmation never changes payment approval state, returns `409` while any batch payment remains undecided, excludes rejected entries from the confirmed amount, and requires at least one already-approved payment. Confirmation/rejection preserves reviewer/time/note metadata, and remittance rejection is replay-safe.
- `backend/collector-reschedules.js`: authenticated reschedule upload/list/paid-resolution and Admin schedule CRUD mounted under `/api/collector/payments/reschedules`. Collector uploads are idempotent by collector/client record ID, validated against customer-area assignments, and never trust a collector identity from the request body. Every schedule read reconciles active records against Billing's canonical ending balance; zero-balance records become audited `Paid` schedule history and therefore disappear from active Admin/Android queues without deleting the audit record. The explicit paid-resolution endpoint independently rechecks the same canonical balance and rejects premature client-side resolution. Partial-payment follow-ups must link to the collector's canonical payment entry and preserve the payment reference, paid amount, remaining balance, date, preferred time, optional note, and audit history. Admin `POST` requires an active Collector account assigned to the selected customer area; Admin `PUT`/`PATCH /:id` changes schedule details without reassigning customer/collector, and partial-payment edits are limited to the follow-up date, preferred time, and optional note; Admin `DELETE /:id` creates a `Deleted` history tombstone so Android Sync can clear cached active reminders.
- `backend/collector-priorities.js`: Admin-only create/edit/cancel plus Admin/collector list access mounted under `/api/collector/payments/priorities`. Admin `POST` accepts one `accountNumber` or up to 200 unique `accountNumbers` and saves the batch atomically after branch, duplicate, and canonical unpaid-balance validation. Assignments use `assignmentScope: all_collectors`; every collector in the branch receives the same queue without an area or named-collector restriction. It enforces one active assignment per client, Urgent/High/Normal/Low levels, a collection date, optional expiration, and a required reason. Canonical Billing ending balance is snapshotted on reads and writes; fully paid assignments automatically become audited `Paid` history, expired records become `Expired`, and Admin deletion becomes retained `Cancelled` history. These records never alter bills, payments, balances, approval state, or area assignments.
- `backend/collector-next-due.js`: resolves account collection timing and is consumed directly by Admin authentication/information flows.
- `backend/routes/collectors.js`: retained dormant relational route implementation; it is not mounted by `server.js` and is available only at its canonical module path.
- The former four repository-root/`routes` backend shims were retired in Phase 11.
- Collector login/session/map/transaction endpoints are implemented in Admin-owned `auth.js` under `/api/auth`.

All API prefixes, collector/admin authentication requirements, feature gates, and response contracts remain unchanged by the physical migration.

## Frontend entry points

- Canonical browser implementations live under `web/`: two HTML entry points, three stylesheets, and two JavaScript files.
- Existing URLs remain `/collectors.html`, `/collectors-history.html`, `/collectors`, and `/collectors-history`.
- `collectors.html` is a one-page Tabler operations workspace. One compact segmented KPI strip summarizes collector coverage, **Total Collected This Month** across all collectors, pending payments, and active reschedules; it uses four columns on desktop and two columns on tablets and phones. The page applies a dedicated compact-density layer with reduced page/card gaps, section padding, heading chrome, controls, table rows, remittance records, and assignment cards while preserving readable labels and all actions. Automatic refresh pauses while the page is hidden or offline, any dialog or field edit is active, a batch/area/priority selection is in progress, or another refresh is running; transient background failures retain the last rendered data, and the page header reports refresh/paused/last-updated state. Each Collector Assignment card keeps **Collected This Month** scoped to that collector's approved payments by actual recorder identity, independent of current assigned areas. The desktop layout uses an 8/4 split: a compact Priority Clients table and Rescheduled Clients occupy the primary column, while the sticky right rail contains compact Customer Payment Approval, Collector Cash Remittance, and the independently scrollable Collector Assignment card grid. Admin create/view/edit/cancel actions and the responsive explicit-close batch modal remain available. The modal lists canonical unpaid clients from highest ending balance to lowest, excludes clients already in the active queue, supports checkbox selection plus Select All Visible/Clear, and applies one priority/date/optional expiration/reason to the atomic batch. Rejection opens an explicit-close Tabler modal with a required 500-character audit reason; successful approval/rejection refreshes both the approval queue and cash-remittance eligibility. Collector Cash Remittance provides Pending/Remitted/Rejected filters, approved-cash and payment-status summaries, an explicit-close review modal, required-reason rejection, and reviewer audit display. Confirm Remitted is disabled while any customer payment remains undecided or when the batch contains no approved payment, and the remittance action never approves a payment. Each assignment card shows collector identity, assigned-area and monthly-collection metrics, up to three area badges plus overflow count, a warning treatment for unassigned collectors, and explicit View Details/Edit controls. Collector search appears only when more than five collectors exist and matches collector identity or assigned area without changing the global KPI totals. Rescheduled Clients keeps Create/Refresh actions in its section header and adds a compact Schedule Filters toggle with an active-filter count; Search, Collector, Status, Schedule Date, and Clear remain collapsed by default, selected values persist while collapsed, and Clear stays inside the expanded panel. Any filter change resets pagination to page one. The section summarizes filter-aware Overdue/Today/Upcoming counts, prioritizes active records by schedule date, and paginates ten records per page. Its desktop table fits the primary column with fixed 14/22/16/26/12/10 percent Schedule/Client/Collector/Details/Status/Actions widths, compact cells, two-line detail clamping, ellipsis tooltips, and narrow centered status/actions; it converts records to cards below tablet width. Each record has an explicit View action, while active records also expose Edit/Delete. The modal shows linked partial-payment details and audit identity; Admins can edit only the follow-up date, preferred time, and optional note for those records. At narrower widths the sections remain in one page and stack responsively.

## Data and dependencies

- Canonical storage, database, and role imports come from `core/`.
- Reschedules use the `collector_followups` data-store key, which writes `data/collector_followups.json` in JSON mode and the protected `app_store` row in MySQL mode.
- Priority assignments use the `collector_priority_assignments` data-store key in the same JSON/protected `app_store` abstraction. Each record stores branch/client/area snapshots, `all_collectors` scope, priority, collection and optional expiration dates, required reason, canonical balance snapshot/check time, lifecycle state, Admin identities, timestamps, and append-only audit history.
- A reschedule stores its server/client IDs, branch, customer/account/area, assigned collector identity, visit result, schedule date/time, notes, lifecycle status, source, creator/updater/deleter audit fields, and timestamps. Partial-payment follow-ups additionally store their immutable canonical payment ID/reference, payment status when scheduled, paid amount, remaining balance, separate optional collector note, and append-only change history. A newer schedule archives the prior active schedule for that account; paid resolution and Admin deletion archive active schedules. Deleted records remain history tombstones for Android reconciliation.
- Depends directly on migrated Admin accounts/roles and collector authentication.
- Depends on Customer Management for customer identity, area, and account number.
- Depends directly on migrated Billing for canonical payment records, entry numbering, balances, and service refresh.
- JSON mode retains approval/rejection audit fields on the collector payment entry. Relational mode stores the same decision in the Collector-owned `collector_payment_reviews` table, created on first review, while `payment_entries.status` remains Billing's effective-entry gate.
- Collector client review uses Billing's canonical Complimentary flag: active exempt subscribers are labeled Complimentary and excluded from area unpaid totals so they are not treated as collection targets. Other collector payment/reschedule contracts are unchanged.
- Remittance and approval data may be consumed by Finance reporting.

## Known risks and follow-up

- Payment capture and approval must remain idempotent and preserve entry numbering. References are still required; stable client payment IDs improve retry identity, while matching legacy retries remain supported for the current Android app.
- Collector authentication routes live outside the module and require Admin/Integration coordination.
- Android releases older than version 1.1 keep reschedules only on-device and cannot populate the Admin table. Version 1.1 uploads immediately when online and retries pending records during assigned-record synchronization.
- The Android offline queue remains device-local until connectivity and a valid collector token are available.
- Admin-created changes appear on Android only after the assigned collector logs in or taps Sync; deleting the server tombstone before that reconciliation would leave stale device state, so tombstones are retained in schedule history.
- Paid schedule cleanup fails safe: if Billing's canonical balance cannot be loaded, the schedule remains active and the explicit resolution endpoint returns a retryable verification error rather than trusting a device balance.
- Priority assignments are replaced from the branch-wide server active list only after a successful Android Sync; a failed request preserves the last device cache. New batches, cancellation, expiration, and full payment appear or disappear independently on each collector device after that device syncs. Concurrent offline collection by different collectors remains an operational risk, but the actual submitting collector is retained on canonical payment/reschedule records and the payment approval/duplicate safeguards remain authoritative.
- Verify permissions for collector versus admin operations after any route change.
- Repository-root backend aliases must not be recreated.
- Add authenticated end-to-end capture, approval, rejection, remittance, reprint, and idempotency tests before changing financial behavior.
